Quell walks the same three miles of farmland every week for a year and writes one essay a month about what changes. Hedges, a flooded lane, a farmer who stops to talk about drainage for an hour.

There is no sermon here. Quell trusts the reader to notice what is being lost, and the restraint makes it land harder than any warning could.
